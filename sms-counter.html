How SMS encoding works

Classic SMS carries 140 bytes. In the GSM-7 alphabet each character takes 7 bits, which fits 160 characters. GSM-7 covers English letters, digits, common punctuation and a set of Western European accented letters. Any character outside it — an emoji, a curly “smart” quote, an en dash, most non-Latin scripts — forces the entire message into UCS-2, a 16-bit encoding that fits only 70 characters.

Carriers and providers bill per segment, so a 150-character promo that picks up one 📦 jumps from 1 segment to 3. Across 10,000 recipients that's 20,000 extra billed segments. The highlighter shows exactly which characters cause it, and the fix button swaps typographic punctuation for plain equivalents.

Practical rules

  • Write in a plain-text editor; word processors insert smart quotes and dashes.
  • Keep transactional SMS under 160 GSM-7 characters where possible.
  • If you need emoji or a non-Latin language, budget for 70/67-character segments.
  • Leave room for opt-out text such as “Reply STOP to opt out,” which many regulations and carriers require.
  • Some providers transliterate or “smart-encode” automatically — check your provider's settings.
