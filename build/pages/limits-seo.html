SEO & search snippet limits

Search engines don't publish a character limit for titles or descriptions — they truncate by pixel width. Character guidelines (50–60 for titles, 150–160 for descriptions) are rules of thumb that hold for typical English text. Technical files such as sitemaps and robots.txt do have documented hard limits.

Titles and descriptions

Desktop titles are commonly measured at about 600 pixels before an ellipsis; descriptions at about 920 pixels on desktop and less on mobile. Wide letters (M, W, capitals) eat space faster than narrow ones (i, l, t). Use the SERP preview for an exact pixel measurement.

Technical limits

  • Each sitemap file: up to 50,000 URLs and 50 MB uncompressed; use a sitemap index beyond that.
  • Sitemap URLs must be under 2,048 characters.
  • Google processes the first 500 KiB of a robots.txt file.

Tips

  • Lead with the query and the benefit; brand last.
  • Write unique titles and descriptions for every indexable page.
  • Alt text: describe the image's purpose in a sentence; around 125 characters is a common accessibility guideline.

FAQ

Is there an official meta description length?

No. Snippets are truncated by width and are often rewritten to match the query. 150–160 characters is a practical desktop target.

Do long titles hurt rankings?

A long title isn't a penalty, but the cut-off part won't be seen and search engines may rewrite it. Put what matters first.

How long should a URL slug be?

Short and descriptive. Under about 75 characters keeps URLs readable and shareable.
