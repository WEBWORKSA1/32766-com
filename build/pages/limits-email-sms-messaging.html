Email, SMS & messaging limits

Messaging limits decide what your customer actually sees — and what you pay. SMS is billed per segment, and one emoji can triple a message's segment count. Email has hard protocol limits (addresses, line length) and soft limits that cost you attention (subject lines cut off in mobile inboxes) or content (Gmail clips large HTML emails).

SMS in one paragraph

Plain messages use GSM-7: 160 characters, or 153 per segment when split. Any character outside GSM-7 switches the whole message to UCS-2: 70 characters, or 67 per segment. Our SMS guide explains the maths and the traps.

Email

  • Addresses: 254 characters total, 64 before the @.
  • Subject lines: ~60 characters show on desktop, 30–40 on phones — front-load the point.
  • Gmail clips messages whose HTML exceeds roughly 102 KB, hiding your footer and unsubscribe link.

Push notifications

APNs and Firebase Cloud Messaging payloads are capped at 4 KB (4,096 bytes), including JSON keys — measure with the byte counter.

FAQ

Why did my 100-character SMS count as 2 messages?

It contains a character outside GSM-7 (often a smart quote, dash or emoji), which switches it to UCS-2 with a 70-character single-message limit.

What is the ideal email subject length?

Short enough that the key words show on mobile — roughly 30–50 characters — while staying under about 60.

How do I stop Gmail clipping my newsletter?

Keep the HTML under ~102 KB: minify code, host images, and remove unused styles.
