Title tag & meta description length

There's no official character limit — snippets are cut by pixel width. Here's how to write titles and descriptions that show in full and earn the click.

32766.com editorial · Updated November 19, 2026 · 6-minute read

Targets
  • Title: about 600 px on desktop — usually 50–60 characters.
  • Description: about 920 px on desktop — usually 140–158 characters; ~120 for mobile.
  • Put the keyword and the benefit first; brand last or not at all.

Why pixels?

Results pages give each title a fixed width in a proportional font. Uppercase letters and wide glyphs (M, W) take up to three times the space of narrow ones (i, l, t). A 58-character title can overflow while a 66-character one fits. Character counts are a proxy; pixel width is what actually decides the cut. Our SERP preview measures your text in the same font and size.

Search engines rewrite

Search engines often replace titles that are too long, stuffed with keywords or mismatched with the page's main heading, and they frequently generate descriptions from page content when that better matches a query. You can't prevent it, but you can make your version the best candidate: accurate, specific, unique per page and aligned with the H1.

A title formula that works

[Primary keyword] — [Specific promise or differentiator] | [Brand]
UTF-8 Byte Counter — Find What's Over 32,766 Bytes | 32766
  • Lead with what the searcher typed.
  • Add one concrete hook: a number, year, format, audience or outcome.
  • Drop the brand if space is tight; the URL shows it anyway.

A description formula

[What it is/does] + [proof or specific detail] + [soft call to action]
"Free UTF-8 byte counter: see real string size, spot multi-byte characters and trim safely to Lucene's 32,766-byte limit. No upload — runs in your browser."

Common mistakes

  • Identical titles across pages (templates with only the brand).
  • All-caps titles — wide letters, and they read as shouting.
  • Descriptions that repeat the title instead of adding information.
  • Truncated promises: the key benefit after the cut-off.
