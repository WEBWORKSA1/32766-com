About

Every system counts text somewhere

32766.com is an independent toolkit and reference that makes text limits visible — so a caption isn't cut, an ad isn't rejected, a text message isn't billed three times and an index doesn't fail at 2 a.m.

Why we built it

Most counters answer one question: how many characters? Real systems ask different ones. X weighs characters, LinkedIn counts UTF-16 units, SMS switches encodings, databases and search engines count bytes, and AI models count tokens. We built one place that counts all of them and checks the result against a curated library of real limits.

What we believe

  • Private by default. Tools run in your browser. We don't upload, log or store your text.
  • Sources over guesses. Limits come from official documentation where it exists, with clear labels for recommendations versus hard limits. See our editorial policy.
  • Free for individuals. The site is funded by advertising, sponsorships, donations and paid services for teams.
  • Honest about uncertainty. Token counts are estimates; snippet previews are approximations. We say so.

The name

32,766 bytes is the largest single term Apache Lucene can index — the limit behind a famous Elasticsearch error. It's a perfect symbol of a limit you don't notice until you hit it. Read the full story.
