Usernames, URLs & domains

Names and addresses have some of the oldest, hardest limits on the internet. A domain label can't exceed 63 characters and a full domain name 253; an email address tops out at 254. Platforms add their own rules for usernames. Choosing a short name early saves pain everywhere it's displayed or typed.

Domains

Internationalized domain names are stored in Punycode (“xn--…”), so a short non-ASCII label can exceed 63 characters once encoded. Short, memorable domains are rare for this reason — numeric five-digit .com names such as this one are a classic example of scarcity.

URLs and cookies

No standard caps URL length, but servers, proxies and CDNs do; keeping URLs under about 2,000 characters is the safe practice. Browsers must support at least 4,096 bytes per cookie under RFC 6265.

FAQ

How long can a domain name be?

Each label (between dots) can be up to 63 characters, and the full name up to 253.

How long can an X handle be?

15 characters: letters, numbers and underscores.

What's the maximum Wi-Fi network name length?

32 bytes — fewer characters if you use emoji or accented letters.
