The number behind the name

What is 32766?

To most people it's just a five-digit number. To anyone who has run a search cluster, it's a wall: 32,766 bytes is the longest single term Apache Lucene will index.

By the 32766.com editorial team · Updated November 19, 2026 · 8-minute read

Key facts
  • 32,766 bytes — maximum length of one indexed term in Apache Lucene, and so in Elasticsearch, OpenSearch and Solr.
  • 32,766 = 215 − 2 = 0x7FFE in hexadecimal = 111111111111110 in binary.
  • 32,767 = 215 − 1, the largest signed 16-bit integer: Excel's characters-per-cell limit, Windows' extended path length and the top of Kubernetes' NodePort range.
  • ZIP code 32766 serves Oviedo (alias Chuluota) in Seminole County, Florida.

1. The Lucene term limit

Lucene breaks text into terms and stores each term in an inverted index. When a value is indexed as a single exact term — an Elasticsearch keyword field, a not_analyzed string in older versions, a flattened field, or any token an analyzer produces — that term must be at most 32,766 bytes of UTF-8. Anything longer is rejected with errors such as:

Document contains at least one immense term in field="description"
(whose UTF8 encoding is longer than the max length 32766), all of which were skipped.
max_bytes_length_exceeded_exception: bytes can be at most 32766 in length; got 51000

The limit is in bytes, not characters. Elasticsearch's ignore_above mapping option, often recommended as the fix, counts characters — so a value of 10,000 emoji (40,000 bytes) can slip past an ignore_above: 10000 guard and still fail. Elastic's current documentation also notes that in newer columnar index modes ignore_above no longer shields you from the byte limit. The complete set of fixes is in our guide to fixing the immense-term error, and you can test any value with the byte counter.

2. Why 32,766 and not 32,768?

Lucene's indexing chain writes terms into in-memory byte blocks of 32,768 bytes (215) and prefixes each term with a length marker of up to 2 bytes. The longest term that fits in one block is therefore 32,768 − 2 = 32,766 bytes — exposed in the code as the maximum term length. The practical lesson is broader than the internals: a limit sits wherever the implementation draws it, frequently a power of two minus a little. Never assume “about 32 KB” — measure exact bytes.

4. The maths of 32766

  • Even and composite: 32,766 = 2 × 3 × 43 × 127.
  • It has 16 divisors and is abundant — its proper divisors add up to more than itself.
  • Binary 111111111111110 (fourteen 1s then a 0); hexadecimal 7FFE; octal 77776.
  • log2(32,766) ≈ 14.9999 — a hair under 15 bits.
  • Read aloud as a domain: “three-two-seven, six-six”.

5. ZIP code 32766

In the United States, 32766 is a postal ZIP code for Oviedo, Florida, with Chuluota as its city alias, in Seminole County east of Orlando. Census-based profiles put its population around 16,000 and its median household income well above the national median. 6. Why limits matter for business

Every channel a business uses has a limit somewhere — a 30-character ad headline, a 160-character SMS, a 600-pixel search title, a 32,766-byte index term, a model's context window. Hitting one silently costs money: truncated messages, rejected ads, double-billed texts, failed indexing jobs, AI answers that ignore half the prompt. 32766.com exists to make those limits visible before they cost you.
