    How words are counted

    A word is any run of letters or digits your browser's language-aware word segmenter recognizes. Hyphenated words (“well-known”) and contractions (“don't”) count as one word in English. For Chinese, Japanese and Thai, which don't put spaces between words, the browser's dictionary-based segmenter splits words for you, so counts are far more accurate than space-splitting tools.

    Reading and speaking time

    Reading time uses 238 words per minute, a widely cited average for silent reading of English non-fiction. Speaking time uses 150 words per minute, a comfortable presentation pace. Technical text reads slower; adjust your expectations accordingly.

    Readability (Flesch reading ease)

    The Flesch score combines sentence length and syllables per word. Higher means easier: 60–70 is plain English, 30–50 is college-level, under 30 is dense. Syllables are estimated with a heuristic, so treat it as a guide rather than a grade.

    FAQ

    Do numbers count as words?

    Yes. “2026” and “32,766” each count as one word, like most word processors.

    Why does my word processor show a different number?

    Tools disagree on hyphens, slashes, URLs and em dashes without spaces. Differences are usually under 1%. If an exact count is required for a submission, use the counter the institution specifies.

    What is keyword density good for?

    It shows which terms dominate your text so you can spot repetition. There is no ideal percentage for SEO; write naturally and cover the topic fully.
